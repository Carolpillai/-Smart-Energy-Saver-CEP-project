01 / Consumption Drivers

Where Household Energy Is Consumed

Appliance power draw varies significantly. Understanding average wattage enables households to target duration adjustments where they yield maximum bill reduction.

High Impact

Air Conditioner

Typical Rating: 1,500 – 2,000 W

Cooling accounts for over 50% of peak summer bills. Set thermostat to 24°C – 26°C (every 1°C temperature increase saves ~6% electricity)!

Medium Impact

Refrigerator

Typical Rating: 150 – 300 W

Runs continuously 24/7. Dirty coils or worn door seals can increase compressor runtime by up to 25%.

High Impact

Water Heater / Geyser

Typical Rating: 2,000 – 3,000 W

Extremely high power draw. Leaving it switched on continuously leads to repeated reheating power cycles.

Low-Med Impact

Ceiling Fan

Typical Rating: 65 – 75 W

Modest power draw, but long daily hours across multiple rooms can accumulate into significant total monthly kWh.

Medium Impact

Washing Machine

Typical Rating: 500 – 800 W

Heating water accounts for up to 80% of wash cycle energy. Cold water loads dramatically cut consumption.

Low Impact

Smart Television

Typical Rating: 80 – 150 W

Set-top boxes and background standby modes continue drawing 5-15W even when the display panel is powered off.

Low Impact

LED Lighting

Typical Rating: 9 – 15 W / bulb

Highly efficient compared to old incandescent bulbs (60W). Upgrading yields immediate, permanent reductions.

Low Impact

Laptop / Computer

Typical Rating: 40 – 250 W

Laptops consume significantly less power than desktop PCs. Using sleep/hibernate settings stops idle drain.

Academic Field Project

Built from the community, for the community.

Smart Energy Saver was developed as a student field project combining empirical survey research, product design, and interactive energy awareness.

Why We Chose This Problem

While global energy discussions focus on industrial grid transformation, everyday household conservation remains an unmapped frontier. Most residents care about high electricity bills but lack intuitive tools to pinpoint which specific habits cause billing spikes.

Our goal was to design an engaging, data-driven prototype that translates abstract electrical units (Watts, kWh) into relatable time and monetary metrics.

Project Execution & Scope

  • Field Research: Conducted primary surveys across [Community / Locality] households.
  • Data Synthesis: Analyzed consumption habits and identified high-impact behavioral intervention points.
  • Digital Tool Design: Engineered an interactive calculator, score engine, and habit simulator.
  • User Testing: Validated usability and clarity with survey participants.
